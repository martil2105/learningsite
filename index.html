The Microeconomics Sequence

An ordered progression through microeconomic foundations. Each article builds on the machinery of the last, connected by a two-sentence bridge explaining what the previous article established and what the next one needs.

Introduction

Trade allows two economies to mutually gain whenever their opportunity costs differ, but market equilibrium leaves the division of surplus entirely to relative size.

Part 01 Principles

Comparative advantage

Two economies both gain at any price between their opportunity costs, but nobody gets to choose the price. Relative size sets it, both gain only inside a band exactly as wide as the gap between their costs, and an economy that is better at everything, trading with a partner its own size, can come away with exactly nothing.

Bridge

Comparative advantage took production techniques as fixed endowments across nations. Economic rent investigates where technological differences originate: innovating firms adopt superior techniques to capture temporary rents, which competition systematically erodes while spreading the innovation.

Part 02 U1–U2

Economic rent

Seven technologies make the same thing, and the rectangle test every first course teaches leaves five survivors — crowning a sensible middle option that is the cheapest choice at no price at all. Rent is the gap under the price, and competition closes it from below while copying the difference that opened it.

Bridge

Economic rent examined how firms select cost-minimising techniques under given market prices. Constrained choice steps back to the individual decision-maker, establishing the foundational geometry of optimization where subjective preferences balance against objective resource constraints at a tangency.

Part 03 U3

Constrained choice

A pay rise makes free time dearer and makes you richer, and every course says theory cannot tell you which effect wins. For the utility function every course draws it does: they cancel to the last digit the arithmetic can carry, at every wage — and what actually shortened the working day was a subsistence floor getting out of the way, not one effect beating the other.

Bridge

Constrained choice solved an isolated agent's trade-offs against fixed environmental limits. Nash equilibrium introduces strategic interaction, where each agent's optimal decision depends on what others do and your own equilibrium behaviour is built entirely from the other player's payoffs.

Part 04 U4

Nash equilibrium

One game in eight has no equilibrium you can find by checking cells. In those games your own behaviour is built entirely from the other player's payoffs — so raising a penalty does not reduce the thing it punishes, it buys less monitoring, at exactly the rate that leaves misconduct where it was.

Bridge

Nash equilibrium analyzed non-cooperative games with fixed simultaneous moves. Bargaining models how two parties divide a shared surplus through alternating offers, proving that impatience and outside options pin down a unique division on the Pareto frontier.

Part 05 U5

Bargaining and the surplus

Any point on the frontier beats no deal, and nothing in the picture picks one — until offers alternate, and then the model picks exactly one, built from the other side's impatience over both, with your outside option worth exactly nothing until it crosses the line.

Bridge

Bargaining resolved bilateral negotiations over a shared surplus. Markup and elasticity examines how a price-setting firm sells to many consumers, showing that the optimal markup is governed by elasticity while demand curvature dictates cost pass-through.

Part 06 U7

Markup and elasticity

markup = 1/|elasticity| is exactly true at every optimum — and it is a statement about a single point. Two demand curves through that point set the same price to the last digit, and one passes half a cost increase through while the other passes two and a half times.

Bridge

Markup and elasticity analyzed a single price-setting firm with monopoly power. Tax incidence shifts to competitive markets with price-taking buyers and sellers, proving that tax burden is determined entirely by relative elasticities rather than statutory liability.

Part 07 U8

Tax incidence

Who writes the cheque changes nothing, to fourteen decimal places; the elasticities set the split exactly for a straight line and only to first order otherwise — and at the revenue-maximising rate the tax has destroyed half the trades and burned half the take.

September 2026Read →
Bridge

Tax incidence demonstrated how competitive supply and demand clear ordinary goods markets. Efficiency wages reveals why the labour market cannot clear like an ordinary market: when effort is unobservable, firms set wages to motivate discipline, generating involuntary unemployment as an equilibrium necessity.

Part 08 U6

Efficiency wages

The wage buys effort, and the cheapest effort sits where the effort elasticity is exactly one — a number labour demand never enters, which is why one worker and a thousand draw the same pay and the queue outside is the collateral, not the failure.

September 2026Read →
Bridge

Efficiency wages demonstrated contractual failure within the employment relationship. Externalities tackles failure across the wider market when private transactions impose uncounted costs on third parties, determining when taxes, quotas, or bargaining best minimize expected social loss under uncertainty.

Part 09 U10

Externalities

A tax, a quota and a bargain reach the same quantity and move the same money into different pockets, so the choice looks purely distributional — until the regulator is uncertain, and then the expected-loss ratio between price and quantity is the square of a slope ratio, crossing at exactly one.

Population Stability Index

Every reading is how far the population moved plus (B−1)(1/N + 1/M), and the second term is there whether anything moved or not. The 0.10 and 0.25 lines ignore it — so on a small segment the traffic light is mostly reporting your sample size, and on a large one it cannot see a shift worth eight points of approval rate.
